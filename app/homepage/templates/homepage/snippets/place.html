It is also the thing the page could not say at all until now: the address sat on the settings row, edited on the staff page, and was rendered by no template anywhere. Every part stands on its own. A troop that has named its hall but uploaded no picture gets the name and the address; one that has filled in nothing gets no band at all, rather than an empty frame where a map should be. The map is the troop's own upload and not an embed, which is a deployment decision rather than a design one: a live map has every visitor's browser call a map service from the unit's own page, and the keyed ones would make each troop open an account with that service before its homepage worked. What the page owes a parent is directions, so it gives them as a link they choose to follow. The map is that link wherever there is one, because the picture of the place is the thing a reader points at when they want to be sent to it; a "Directions" line beside it would be a second, quieter copy of the same offer.
